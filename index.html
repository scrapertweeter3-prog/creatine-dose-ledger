Creatine is priced like a coffee and dosed like one

The supplement aisle sells tubs while the body takes grams, and the shelf price hides the conversion. A 500g tub at $15 works out to 3 cents a gram. The standard dose is 5 grams a day, so a day costs $0.15 and a year costs $54.75, because 1,825 grams a year comes to 3.65 tubs. Rounded up to whole tubs the budget line reads $60.

The loading phase sounds like the expensive part and is not. 20 grams a day for 5 days is 100 grams, $3 of powder, once. What costs money that week is the branding around it. Pick one store brand, write the tub price on the lid, and the only variable left in the ledger is the dose itself; the effect sizes live in the reviews, where they belong.

Set the year against a coffee habit. A $6 latte every day is $2,190 a year, 40 times the creatine budget, and the decade split is $21,900 against $547.50. Both columns ride on assumptions, $15 a tub and $6 a cup, and the ranking holds across a wide range of them: even at $30 a tub the year is $109.50, still under six weeks of lattes at $252.

Per-day pricing keeps a supplement honest with the rest of the protocol. The sauna carries a cost per session, the tracker a cost per night, and creatine lands at $0.15 a day, the smallest line on the page. Price the grams first and let the reviews argue about the rest.
